General Supportive-Listening Principles

Validation means acknowledging that a person's emotional reaction makes sense given their situation, without necessarily agreeing with every belief behind it. Specific validation ("it makes sense you'd feel anxious with three deadlines in one week") lands better than generic validation ("that sounds hard").

Asking one open-ended question at a time, and letting the person finish their thought before offering advice, tends to make people feel heard rather than managed. Jumping straight to solutions before someone has finished describing the problem is a common way well-intentioned support can feel dismissive.

Normalizing a struggle ("a lot of students go through something like this around this point in the semester") can reduce shame, but should not be used to minimize what the person is currently feeling.

Peer-support and AI-based support tools are appropriate for everyday stress, venting, and light problem-solving. They are not a substitute for a licensed counselor when someone shows signs of a persistent mental health condition, or expresses thoughts of self-harm — those situations call for a warm handoff to professional or crisis resources, not continued informal support alone.

Encouraging small, concrete next steps (e.g. "would it help to write down just tomorrow's top priority?") is generally more useful than broad advice like "try not to stress so much," which can feel dismissive of how real the stress is.
